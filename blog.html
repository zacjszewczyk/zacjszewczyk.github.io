Detecting & Responding to AI-aided or Agentic Attacks

By Zac Szewczyk on 2026/11/19 08:51:33 EST in Cybersecurity

Michael Tigges in Detecting & Responding to AI-aided or Agentic Attacks:

“Correcting a path or adjusting an argument requires very little work. Changing how a tool handles errors, incorporates discoveries, and orders subsequent operations requires more. As the complexity and novelty of the change increase, so does the amount of effort a human would ordinarily need to implement it. That’s the relationship I care about: new information, the complexity of the resulting adaptation, and the time between the two. A prepared workflow has already paid much of that implementation cost. When the activity repeatedly accommodates new details and unfamiliar failures within short intervals, I start considering how an LLM could be supplying that adaptation as the operation progresses.”

He has a slew of good advice for investigations in general, whether or not AI plays a role. And while I think it’s not helpful to hunt for only activity involving AI, as if C2 bears some indelible watermark if it involves AI in any form, understanding some of the tells that might indicate AI was used could be helpful when pulling the investigative thread.

You Don't Need AI, You Need an Algorithm

By Zac Szewczyk on 2023/12/04 22:06:36 EST in Tech

A few weeks ago, I participated in a working group tasked with identifying areas where artificial intelligence and machine learning could improve network utilization. What, specifically, “improve” and “utilization” meant were some of the first questions we addressed. During that conversation, though, one of the participants made an insightful observation that the group promptly ignored. He told a brief story about how someone had asked him for a networking device that could aggregate multiple uplinks and then “use AI to choose the best one.” When he offered a simple WAN failover solution, a technology that has existed for decades and comes built-in to open source firewalls like pfSense, commercial appliances from the likes of Cisco, and every modern mobile phone, the customer told him, “No.” Like our working group, that customer person had a solution (artificial intelligence) driving their requirements (an AI-based WAN failover), not the other way around.

The current version of this project is, for the most part, a direct NodeJS-to-Python port of Matthew’s code from 2022 with a few minor changes, but I look forward to building on it and keeping pace with Matthew.
